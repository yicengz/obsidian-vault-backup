Elon Musk on Saturday said reports of his space exploration and technology company SpaceX (SPACE) raising money at an $800B valuation were "not accurate." The billionaire businessman also said that frequent contractor NASA would make up less than 5% of the company's revenue next year.

The Wall Street Journal on Friday said SpaceX (SPACE) was preparing a secondary share sale that would value the rocket manufacturer at $800B, citing people familiar with the matter. That number would have made SPACE the most valuable private company in the world. 

"SpaceX has been cash flow positive for many years and does periodic stock buybacks twice a year to provide liquidity for employees and investors," Musk said on his X account.

"Valuation increments are a function of progress with Starship and Starlink and securing global direct-to-cell spectrum that greatly increases our addressable market," he added.

Starlink Is Cash Cow

Musk separately expressed "great fondness" for NASA but said the agency would "constitute less than 5% of our revenue next year."

"Commercial Starlink is by far our largest contributor to revenue," the world's richest man added.

Musk also pushed back against what he called some people's claims that SpaceX (SPACE) gets subsidies from NASA. "This is absolutely false," he said. 



"The SpaceX team won the NASA contracts because we offered the best product at the lowest price. BOTH best product AND lowest cost. With regard to astronaut transport, SpaceX is currently the only option that passes NASA safety standards," Musk added.
